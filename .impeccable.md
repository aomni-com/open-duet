## Design Context

### Users

Duet is for founders and small teams who delegate consequential work to an always-on AI coworker. They move repeatedly between starting work, resuming durable sessions, inspecting files, checking scheduled jobs, and managing integrations. The interface is used for long stretches, so it must remain calm under density and make state legible without demanding attention.

### Brand Personality

Composed, capable, and quietly warm. Duet should feel precise enough for operational work, personable without becoming toy-like, and premium through restraint rather than ornament.

### Aesthetic Direction

The product is a refined operational workspace built from three perceptually related structural planes:

1. **The global header** is the strongest structural plane: the warmest and slightly darkest chrome in light mode, and the most lifted chrome in dark mode. It frames the product without becoming the subject.
2. **Contextual navigation** uses a quieter intermediate green-neutral. It organizes sessions and tools while remaining clearly subordinate to the global header.
3. **The working canvas** is the cleanest plane: a near-white tinted surface in light mode and the deepest low-chroma surface in dark mode. This is where conversation and files become the subject.

Raised controls and grouped objects use card or popover surfaces only when they represent a real interaction boundary. Most hierarchy should come from alignment, spacing, and lightness—not nested containers.

The memorable quality is the quiet transition from warm chrome to focused canvas. Boundaries are hairlines with reduced opacity; shadows are rare and shallow. Blue is a scarce signal reserved for an active destination, a live state, focus, or the primary next action. Duet's illustrated 2D identity supplies warmth while the surrounding interface stays disciplined.

Both light and dark themes are first-class. Shared semantic OKLCH tokens own the palette, and neutral chroma falls as surfaces approach white or black. The interface should never look pure gray, purple-tinted, glassy, or generically “AI.”

### Surface and Contrast Rules

- The sidebar and canvas must be distinguishable at a glance without requiring a heavy divider. A small lightness step and a low-opacity one-pixel boundary are enough.
- The header owns a dedicated semantic surface. Its contrast step is stronger than the sidebar boundary, but it uses a hairline rather than a shadow.
- Sidebar text derives from the sidebar tone; avoid washed-out generic gray on colored chrome.
- A selected row may use a restrained blue wash and inset ring. Resting and hover rows stay neutral.
- Cards are for agents, prompts, menus, and other true objects—not for every section. Never stack multiple decorative cards to manufacture hierarchy.
- Composer surfaces may float through contrast and a shallow shadow, but the page must not draw a rule across the full width merely to announce the composer.
- Dark mode uses a deep canvas with slightly lifted chrome and controls. It should preserve the same hierarchy as light mode rather than invert into glowing accents.

### Density and Rhythm

- Operational rows are compact on desktop and retain full touch targets on mobile.
- Use a four-point spacing rhythm with tight spacing inside a concept and larger gaps between concepts.
- Section labels are quiet orientation aids, not visual headlines. Conversation titles and the current task carry the strongest type weight.
- Align avatars, row text, timestamps, and composer content to a small set of shared vertical axes.
- Empty states should feel intentional and immediately actionable, with little explanatory copy and one dominant input.

### Design Principles

- **The work is the subject.** Chrome orients and recedes; conversation, files, and active tasks remain dominant.
- **Contrast comes from relationships.** Tune sidebar, canvas, card, border, and text together instead of changing one color in isolation.
- **Blue is precious.** Use it for active, live, focused, or primary—not decoration.
- **Density can still breathe.** Compact rows and restrained typography should be grouped with deliberate negative space.
- **Progressive depth, not permanent decoration.** Reveal emphasis through hover, focus, selection, and open states.
- **Duet provides the personality.** Use the real 2D avatar; avoid generic robot marks or ornamental AI motifs.
- **Every state is real.** Loading, empty, error, and active treatments must represent existing data and APIs—never mocks in production UI.

### Anti-References

- Purple or cyan “AI” tinting, neon dark mode, decorative gradients, and glow.
- Generic dashboard card grids, excessive rounded containers, and repeated drop shadows.
- Heavy full-width separators where surface contrast already establishes structure.
- Oversized type, low-density session rows, or explanatory empty states that delay the next action.
