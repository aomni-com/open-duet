# Source to Social

A post begins with an evidence record, not a catchy sentence. `AGENTS.md` owns the publishing boundary, files, research budget, cadence, and approval rule; this skill governs the transformation from source to original organic creative.

## Build the evidence unit

Create or update one row in `hooks.md` before writing copy:

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Hook ID | `H-###`, stable across every post that uses it |
| Source | Exact URL opened or internal artifact path checked |
| Source date | Publication or product-evidence date |
| Found | Date retrieved |
| Direct observation | The narrow fact, quote, demonstration, or metrics snapshot actually present |
| Original contribution | What the company knows, shows, or argues that the source does not |
| Status | `ADMITTED`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `STALE`, or `REJECTED` |
| Used by | Post IDs, or `none` |

`ADMITTED` means the source was opened, identity and date match, the direct observation is accurately described, and the company has a distinct contribution. Missing any one of those makes it `HYPOTHESIS`, not an invitation to write around the gap.

## Transform without laundering

Write these four lines in order:

1. **Observed:** what the source directly shows.
2. **Relevant because:** why the named audience would care; label this analysis unless the source says it.
3. **We can add:** the company's proof, experience, demonstration, or defensible argument.
4. **Hook:** a compact invitation into that contribution, without inflating the observation.

Do not let the source supply both subject and expression. After drafting, compare the opening, sequence, examples, metaphor, and payoff with the source. If three or more are substantially the same, rebuild the post around the company's contribution. Swapping synonyms is still copying.

When exact source language carries the point, quote no more than necessary, name the creator or publisher in the copy, and retain the direct link. Attribution does not convert a full-post rewrite into original work.

## Choose the post shape

Match the contribution to the lightest form that can prove it:

- **Text post:** one claim whose explanation fits without a visual demonstration.
- **Image or carousel:** a comparison, sequence, framework, or artifact the audience needs to inspect.
- **Short video:** motion, transformation, a product interaction, or a narrated demonstration genuinely adds meaning.
- **Thread or series:** the idea contains several independently useful claims; do not compress them into a caption list.

Video is not the default reward for finding an engaging hook. If the evidence is textual and the generated visual would be decorative stock motion, choose text or a real artifact instead.

## Build short video from proof

Write the build sheet before generating anything:

```markdown
Evidence on screen: <real product, artifact, data, or clearly illustrative visual>
0–2s: <visual action and hook text>
2–8s: <problem or contrast>
8–20s: <company contribution or demonstration>
20–30s: <payoff and one CTA>
Voiceover: <complete 45–70 word script>
Captions: <verbatim narration or intentionally shorter text>
Synthetic elements: <what is generated and what it must not imply>
```

Prefer a real screen recording, product shot, chart, or licensed brand asset whenever it can carry the claim. A generated clip may illustrate a metaphor or bridge a cut; it cannot demonstrate that a product works, depict a customer outcome, or fabricate a human reaction.

## Platform pass

For each platform, preserve the claim and change the delivery:

- Rewrite the opening for the platform's reading or viewing context.
- Keep attribution and claim limits intact.
- Use only conventions the brand actually uses; do not add trend slang, emojis, or hashtags by reflex.
- Make the CTA a natural next action for the post's objective, not generic “thoughts?” bait.
- Record each final version separately in the post package so approval is exact.

## Pre-approval check

The package may move to `READY FOR APPROVAL` only when all are true:

- the hook ID is `ADMITTED` and not stale;
- the direct observation is narrower than or equal to what the source supports;
- analysis and hypotheses are visibly labelled;
- the company adds something the source does not;
- claims, scarcity, testimonials, and engagement language have evidence;
- copy is materially distinct or explicitly quoted and attributed;
- synthetic media cannot be mistaken for real proof;
- final text and every asset have stable versions.

This check does not approve or publish anything. It produces a reviewable package for the human named in `brief.md`.
