# Follow-Up Emails

**Goal**: Maintain momentum without being annoying. Add value with every touch.

## Required Context

Before drafting, ensure you have:

- What the previous interaction was
- What's changed since then (if anything)
- What you're trying to achieve
- How many times you've already followed up

## Core Rule

**Every follow-up must add value.** Don't just "check in" — give them a reason to engage.

## Follow-Up Types

### The Thoughtful Bump

Use when: First follow-up after no response

```
[Reference your previous message briefly]

[Add something new: insight, resource, or updated context]

[Simple question to restart dialogue]
```

Keep under 50 words. Don't repeat your original email.

### The Clarification

Use when: They might not have understood your relevance

```
[Acknowledge your previous outreach]

[Reframe what you do in simpler terms]

[Connect more clearly to their situation]

[Question to confirm fit]
```

### The Value-Add

Use when: You have something genuinely useful to share

```
[Share a resource, article, or insight relevant to them]

[Brief explanation of why you thought of them]

[Optional soft ask]
```

This works because you're giving before asking.

### The Trigger-Based

Use when: Something changed on their end

```
[Reference the change you noticed (funding, hire, announcement)]

[Connect it to your previous conversation]

[Question about whether priorities shifted]
```

### The Breakup

Use when: Final follow-up before stopping outreach

```
[Acknowledge you've reached out several times]

[Assume timing is the issue, not interest]

[Offer to pause outreach]

[Leave door open for future]
```

```
I've reached out a few times and haven't heard back —
I'll assume the timing isn't right.

I'll stop reaching out, but if [topic] becomes a priority
later, I'm happy to reconnect.
```

## Spacing Guidelines

- First follow-up: 3-5 days after initial
- Subsequent: 5-7 days apart
- Maximum touches: 4-6 before breakup
- Vary your approach each time

## What to Avoid

- "Just checking in"
- "Just following up"
- "Bumping this to the top of your inbox"
- Repeating your first email verbatim
- Getting passive-aggressive
- Following up daily

## When to Stop

Send a breakup email when:

- 4-6 attempts with no response
- Clear signal they're not interested
- Context has changed significantly

It's not forever — you can re-engage later when something changes.
